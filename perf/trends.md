# TikTok trend recipes for CertTrack

Refreshed weekly by the "CertTrack trend refresh" scheduled task (last refresh: 2026-11-16).
Each recipe takes a format that is getting big views on TikTok right now and gives it a CertTrack twist.
The posting agent uses one of these on at least every other post and logs its name in the "trend" field.
Rules: the joke or the useful bit comes first and CertTrack comes second (or not at all). Never invent stats,
fines or customer stories. Most recipes are carousels (slideshow) because carousels are what's working for us.

## Hot right now (seen in Nov 13-16 2026 trend roundups)

### its-november (NEW)
Trend: "It's November... you know what that means" then a cut to the holiday-season reveal (Nov 13 roundup; usually on a famous holiday-song audio). Text only: don't name, picture or quote the singer or use that audio; let TikTok auto music pick. Retire after Nov 30.
CertTrack spin: carousel. Hook: "It's November... you know what that means." -> "Pie orders." / "Holiday hires who need their food handler cards." / "Finding out which permits expire Dec 31." / "Asking who has the login to renew the business license." Last slide: "It's time. (to check the renewal dates)."
Slide kinds: hook, statement x3-4, optional cta. Industry swaps: bakery, retailer, event venue, snow-plow landscaper.

### day-1-survival-story (NEW)
Trend: "Day 1. You're stuck in..." second-person survival story told day by day in a familiar place, ending on a cliffhanger (zombie-outbreak stories, Nov 13 roundup; told with text or illustrations, no people filmed).
CertTrack spin: carousel, one day per slide, "you" voice. Hook: "Day 1. You're the only one in the office who knows when the insurance renews." -> "Day 3. Someone asks for the COI. You find three. None of them say 2026." / "Day 7. The renewal email went to someone who left in March." / "Day 12. You find the login on a sticky note under the keyboard." / "Day 14. The inspector's car pulls into the lot." Last slide: "Day 15: ..." (cliffhanger; caption asks "what happens next?").
Slide kinds: hook, statement x4-5 (title = "Day N.", body = the beat), no product line on the slides. Industry swaps: restaurant GM, dental office manager, fleet dispatcher.

### email-is-down (NEW)
Trend: work-humor posts about email/work apps going down: "Email is down, so I'm now: ..." lists and "new international holiday" rules (#outlook / #outlookdown among the top hashtags the week of Nov 16). Don't use any company's logo; "email is down" is enough. Use only while outage posts are still climbing.
CertTrack spin: carousel. Hook: "Email is down. New office holiday. Rules:" -> "1. No one can send you 'URGENT: updated COI needed today.'" / "2. Renewal notices can't get lost in your inbox. They're lost somewhere else." / "3. You finally open the paper mail. The bond renewal was in there." Last slide: "Email comes back at 2 PM. So does the COI request."
Slide kinds: hook, point x3, optional cta. Alt hook: "Email is down, so I'm now: ..." with 3 suspiciously productive office-manager tasks.

### exactly-what-to-say
Trend: "When <hard moment> happens, here's exactly what to say: '<phrase>'": a verbatim script instead of advice. Still the #1 business/marketing format on Nov 15 (median ~30x usual reach, up from ~19x on Nov 8).
CertTrack spin: carousel. Hook: "When a client asks for your COI and you're not sure it's current, here's exactly what to say:" -> "'Let me pull the current one and send it over today.'" / "Then actually check the policy dates before you hit send." / "If it lapsed: 'Our renewal is in process, I'll send the updated certificate as soon as it's issued.'" Last slide: "Save this for the next 4:55 PM Friday request."
Slide kinds: hook, statement x3 (title = the line to say, body = the note), optional cta. Other scripts: front desk when an inspector walks in early, office manager when a vendor's insurance expired mid-job. Keep it practical; no legal advice.

### money-cant-buy-happiness
Trend: "Money can't buy happiness, but..." finished with a small, specific luxury, one per beat (Nov 2 roundup; pre-Black Friday). Not in Nov 13-16 sources: last call, retire next refresh unless it resurfaces.
CertTrack spin: carousel, one "but it can buy" per slide. Hook: "Money can't buy happiness, but it can buy:" -> "A reminder 60 days before the license expires, not the day after." / "Every COI in one folder you can actually find." / "A Friday where nobody says 'wait, when does our insurance renew?'" Last slide: "Small business luxuries are weird."
Slide kinds: hook, statement x4-5, optional cta.

### tiny-text-reveal
Trend: the real message hides in very small text so viewers pause or zoom (Nov 2 roundup). Not in Nov 13-16 sources: last call.
CertTrack spin: carousel. Hook: "Our office's very calm and organized renewal process:" -> point slide with big title "Everything is under control." and fine print: "the liquor license renews in 9 days and nobody has the login." / "Our insurance is all set." fine print: "(we think. it was set in 2024)". Last slide: "Zoom in on your own paperwork before the inspector does."
Slide kinds: hook, point x3 (big title = calm claim, "fine" key = tiny confession), optional cta. Caption: "zoom in on slide 2".

### kinda-chic
Trend: "Kinda chic to..." captions naming proud but unglamorous habits (Sept meme, on Nov 1 meme lists; not in Nov 13-16 sources: last call).
CertTrack spin: carousel. Hook: "Kinda chic to:" -> "Know when your business license expires without checking." / "Have the COI ready before the GC asks." / "Have one spreadsheet. Not 'FINAL (2)'." Last slide: "Also kinda chic to find out from a reminder, not an inspector."
Slide kinds: hook, statement x4-5, optional cta.

### grr-mondays
Trend: "#GrrMondays": a single true Monday moment stamped "Grr Mondays" (Oct's top meme; not in Nov 13-16 sources: last call, Monday Nov 23 is its final use).
CertTrack spin: carousel of tiny Monday disasters, each ending "Grr Mondays." Hook: "Office manager Monday, 8:02 AM:" -> "Inbox: 47 unread. One says 'URGENT: updated COI needed today.' Grr Mondays." / "The bond renewal notice was in Friday's mail. Nobody opened Friday's mail. Grr Mondays." Last slide: "Make Monday the day the reminder shows up, not the deadline."
Slide kinds: hook, statement x3-4, optional cta. Post on Mondays only.

## Evergreen formats that keep performing

### pov-panic
"POV: <a specific, relatable moment of panic>". The first slide is the POV line and the rest is the story. Our POV carousel was a top post.

### tell-me-without-telling-me
"Tell me you run a <business> without telling me you run a <business>" -> 3-4 inside-joke slides (e.g. contractors: "4 different COIs for 4 different GCs, all expiring in different months").

### red-flags-green-flags
"Red flags when a business says its paperwork is 'handled'" then flip to green flags. The myth slide kind works well for this (red flag = myth, green flag = fact).

### things-that-just-make-sense
"Things that just make sense as a small business owner": 4-6 short satisfying habits ("renewal dates in ONE place", "a reminder 30 days out, not 3").

### rate-it
"Rating ways businesses track license renewals, 1-10": sticky note on the monitor 2/10, memory 0/10, spreadsheet 5/10 ("if someone opens it"), calendar reminders 6/10, a system that alerts the whole team 10/10. Ends with a light CertTrack mention.

### unpopular-opinion
"Unpopular opinion: <a debatable but defensible take>" plus 2-3 supporting slides, and the caption asks "agree or disagree?". Must be genuinely arguable, not rage bait.

### self-aware-brand (Gen Z marketing parody)
The brand roasts its own marketing: "Our marketing team (it's one guy) said to make a TikTok about license renewals. So here's 3 renewals you're definitely forgetting." Honest about being tiny; that's the charm. At most once a week.

### this-or-that
2-4 slides of "Would you rather: renew 30 days early, or explain to the client why the COI expired?" Caption: "comment your picks".
